    Three moves. No windows.

    DropDial lives in the menu bar / system tray and wakes up only when you need it, in the middle of a drag you were already making.

    1. 1

      Start dragging

      Select one or more files on the desktop or in File Explorer/Finder and start dragging, as if you were moving them.

    2. 2

      Press a shortcut

      Shift opens formats, Ctrl + Shift opens tools. F8 and F9 work too. Once the wheel is open you can let go of the keys.

    3. 3

      Release on a petal

      Drop on the format or tool you want. The new file appears next to the original, which is never overwritten.

    Real tools when you need them

    Tools that need settings open in their own window with a live preview. Every audio and video tool has a player with a seek bar, and trimming gives you draggable start and end handles.

    Every job has its own controls

    • Pause and resume. Pause freezes the whole job, including FFmpeg, and stops the clock. Resume carries on exactly where it stopped.
    • Cancel. Stops the job and removes its unfinished files. Your originals are never touched.
    • Hide. Tucks the card away while the job keeps running, and a tray notification tells you when it finishes.
    • Two at a time. Two jobs run in parallel and the rest wait their turn. Long encodes are never cut off.

    Your files stay on your PC

    Online converters ask you to upload your photos, contracts and recordings to someone else's server. DropDial does all of the work on your own machine.

    • Nothing is uploaded. Every conversion runs locally and works offline.
    • Originals are never overwritten. Outputs get a clear suffix, and duplicates get a number.
    • Everything is included. FFmpeg, Python, 7-Zip and system engines ship inside the download.
    • It stays out of the way. No main window, no installer, no startup entries. Quit from the tray/menu bar at any time.

    Questions

    Is DropDial safe to run?

    DropDial is an open source project under the MIT License with full source code available on GitHub. You can verify SHA-256 checksums on the GitHub release page.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. DropDial is open source under the MIT License, with no ads, accounts or paid tiers. Bundled components keep their own licenses.

    Are my files uploaded anywhere?

    No. Everything is processed on your computer by the engines bundled in the download, and DropDial works with no internet connection.

    Why is the download package self-contained?

    It includes everything it needs to run: native engines, portable Python with libraries, and FFmpeg. You don't have to install anything else.

    Where do the converted files go?

    Into the same folder as the original, with a suffix such as photo-converted.jpg. If that name is taken, a number is added. Originals are never changed. Right-click the menu bar / tray icon to open the folder of your latest output.

    What language is supported?

    Yes. DropDial is fully in English.

    Do I need Microsoft Office?

    Only for layout-exact exports of Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, such as PDF or images. DropDial drives your installed Office to do these. Without Office, you can still extract text and table data from modern Office files.

    How do I update or uninstall it?

    Quit DropDial from its menu bar / tray menu. To update, extract the new version into a fresh folder. To uninstall, delete the folder. DropDial adds no installer entries or startup items.
